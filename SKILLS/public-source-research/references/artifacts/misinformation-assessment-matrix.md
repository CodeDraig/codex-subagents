# Misinformation Assessment Matrix

## Claim Classification

| Class | Evidence test | Common mistake |
| --- | --- | --- |
| False | Reliable evidence directly contradicts the material claim. | Treating lack of official confirmation as contradiction. |
| Misleading | Some facts are accurate, but framing, omission, scale, chronology, or causality creates a materially wrong impression. | Fact-checking isolated words while ignoring the implied conclusion. |
| Unverified | Available evidence cannot yet establish or refute the claim. | Converting uncertainty into likely true or likely false without support. |
| Out of context | Authentic material is attached to the wrong event, date, place, speaker, or surrounding sequence. | Calling authentic media fabricated. |
| Satire | The original context signals parody or fiction. | Ignoring deceptive recirculation after context is stripped. |
| Manipulated media | Material content was altered, synthesized, spliced, or deceptively edited. | Treating routine compression or cropping as material manipulation. |
| Coordinated influence | Multiple actors show evidence of planned or centrally enabled distribution. | Inferring coordination from popularity, shared views, or common timing alone. |
| Ordinary error | A supportable mistake lacks evidence of intentional deception or organized distribution. | Assigning motive from outcome. |

Use more than one class when the evidence supports a mixed assessment.

## Amplification Severity

| Level | Signals | Correction posture |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low | Limited reach, low harm, little continued circulation. | Correct the record where encountered; avoid introducing the claim to new audiences. |
| Moderate | Meaningful reach or confusion, but bounded harm and available evidence. | Publish concise context for the exposed audience with primary sourcing. |
| High | Vulnerable audience, reputational or civic harm, manipulated media, or rapid cross-platform spread. | Coordinate evidence, standards, timing, and non-amplifying language before release. |
| Acute | Imminent physical harm, targeted harassment, emergency interference, or a correction likely to worsen danger. | Stop routine analysis and escalate to authorized safety, editorial, or legal owners. |

Set case-specific observable thresholds for reach, velocity, affected audience, demonstrated harm, and correction penetration before assigning a level. Record reassessment times. Downgrade only when evidence shows reduced circulation, reduced harm, or effective correction reach; publication of a correction alone is not decay evidence.

## Correction Selection

- Lead with the verified fact or missing context, not the false framing.
- Match distribution to the audience already exposed.
- Use the original false wording only when identification requires it.
- Link primary evidence and state what remains unknown.
- Avoid motive or coordination claims unless separately supported.
- Record whether silence, a quiet update, a direct reply, a contextual explainer, or a prominent correction creates the least additional harm.
