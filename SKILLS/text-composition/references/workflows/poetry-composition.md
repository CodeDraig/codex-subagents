# Poetry Composition

## Purpose

Compose and revise complete poems, sequences, or lyrics with deliberate relations among sound, syntax, image, line, stanza, and form.

## Intake

Capture requested form or freedom, length, language, speaker, image/material constraints, sound or metrical requirements, performance/music context if supplied, and what must survive a revision.

## Workflow

1. Identify the poem’s pressure: an image, rhythm, argument, address, contradiction, narrative fragment, or procedural constraint. Generate language through that pressure rather than explaining a theme in broken prose.
2. Write the complete requested poem or sequence. Make decisions about line endings, syntax across lines, stanza turns, repetition, image development, and the speaker’s relation to the addressee.
3. For a named fixed form, inspect its requested rules and the user’s deviations. Count lines/stanzas, examine rhyme and stress patterns where required, and label uncertain scansion rather than claiming mechanical perfection.
4. Revise by testing sound aloud or subvocally, alternative line breaks, abstract language, image interactions, and the consequences of cuts. Preserve intentional disfluency, silence, dialect, and visual spacing.
5. Return the poem itself and only the craft notes useful for assessing constraints or revision. For lyrics, distinguish text-level singability estimates from a performed fit to a supplied melody.

Read [poetry-composition-kit.md](../artifacts/poetry-composition-kit.md) when preparing the working record or checking the example against a similar request.

## Decision Rules

- Free verse still makes line and rhythm choices; it is not required to adopt meter or rhyme.
- Repeated words may structure attention or feeling; do not delete them solely as redundancy.
- A strict requested form is a checkable constraint; a deliberately broken form should be described honestly.
- Do not infer syllable or stress certainty from spelling alone, especially across dialects or languages.

## Verification

- Count explicit line/stanza limits and inspect any prescribed refrain or rhyme pattern.
- Read for sound, syntactic ambiguity, image development, and whether line endings earn their emphasis.
- State which metrical/performance checks were actually done; do not claim a recording or sung trial occurred.

## Output Contract

Return exactly: `Poem`, `Form And Sound Notes`, `Revision Decisions`, `Constraint Checks`, `Open Questions`, `Files Changed`, `Handoffs`.
Include supplied source or manuscript locations where relevant; distinguish applied changes, recommendations, unverified details, and coverage still outstanding. Put the requested text in the text section or identify the actual output file.

## Handoffs

Use `rhetorical-style-editor` for a separate language pass, `text-translator` for translation, and `script-development-editor` when a performance script or production cue sheet is requested.

## Boundaries

Full poems, sequences, lyrics, and rewrites are supported. Preserve requested form and source attribution; do not fabricate performance evidence or guarantee musical fit without the relevant material.
