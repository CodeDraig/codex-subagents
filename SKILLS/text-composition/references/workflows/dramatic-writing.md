# Dramatic Writing

## Purpose

Write complete dramatic scenes or scripts in which playable action, speech, silence, and staging carry conflict or another chosen theatrical structure.

## Intake

Capture medium, audience, runtime/length, cast, setting and production limits, character relationships, scene context, formatting needs, and permitted changes to an existing script.

## Workflow

1. Map each participant’s immediate objective, leverage, withheld knowledge, and response to resistance. For non-conflict or experimental drama, identify the alternative organizing action or pattern.
2. Draft complete scenes using playable actions, changing tactics, subtext, entrances/exits, and meaningful silence. Let exposition arise from circumstances rather than having characters recite shared facts.
3. Write stage directions that communicate necessary action, space, or sound without prescribing every emotion or inventing unavailable production resources.
4. Track who is present, what they know, physical objects, scene transitions, and how the situation changes. Distinguish dramatic text from camera plans or recording logistics.
5. Revise dialogue for distinct voices, speakability, cue clarity, pacing, and the ending beat. Estimate timing from text and pauses only as an estimate unless a read-through is supplied or performed.

Read [dramatic-writing-kit.md](../artifacts/dramatic-writing-kit.md) when preparing the working record or checking the example against a similar request.

## Decision Rules

- A character’s stated desire may differ from the action pursued; make the tension playable rather than explaining it in a note.
- Do not add cast, locations, or effects that violate the supplied production brief.
- A screenplay or audio drama may need medium-specific cues; only add the format rules relevant to the requested medium.

## Verification

- Check cast/scene labels, speaker ownership, entrances/exits, prop continuity, and fixed staging constraints.
- Read dialogue for accidental ambiguity and unplayable directions.
- Distinguish an attempted action from a completed movement. Reconcile directions that move an object and then say it did not move, or otherwise give incompatible physical states.
- Compare estimated runtime with the brief and mark the absence of performance evidence.

## Output Contract

Return exactly: `Dramatic Text`, `Character And Action Notes`, `Staging And Continuity`, `Timing And Checks`, `Open Questions`, `Files Changed`, `Handoffs`.
Include supplied source or manuscript locations where relevant; distinguish applied changes, recommendations, unverified details, and coverage still outstanding. Put the requested text in the text section or identify the actual output file.

## Handoffs

Use `script-development-editor` for video/audio cue and production-script refinement, `recording-preparation-coordinator` for capture logistics, and `fiction-development-editor` for larger story architecture.

## Boundaries

Do not claim a script was rehearsed or production-ready without evidence. Full dramatic drafting is supported; actual participant contact, casting, or production commitments are separate actions.
