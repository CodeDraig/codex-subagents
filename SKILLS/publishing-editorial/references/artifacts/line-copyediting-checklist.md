# Line Copyediting Checklist

- Capture edit level, audience, house style, dialect, file format, tracked-change expectations, and author voice constraints.
- Check grammar, punctuation, spelling, capitalization, hyphenation, numerals, citations, headings, lists, tables, figure callouts, and cross-references.
- Review clarity, concision, rhythm, transitions, repetition, jargon, inclusive language, dialogue punctuation, and point-of-view or tense consistency.
- Maintain style-sheet entries for names, places, invented terms, abbreviations, measurements, chronology, capitalization, spelling variants, and exceptions.
- Query ambiguity, factual uncertainty, continuity conflicts, missing references, permissions issues, and author-owned choices.
- Separate applied edits, recommended edits, proof blockers, and downstream handoffs.

## Working Record

- Style entry: term/feature | decision | evidence/house rule | intentional exception | locations.
- Query: location | original wording | ambiguity/factual issue | proposed handling | author decision.
- Change record: source/version | before | after | meaning check | applied/recommended | recheck.

## Worked Example

The narrator repeats “I remember” deliberately, but a date changes from 1978 to 1987 without explanation. Preserve the repeated opening, query the conflicting date, and apply agreed punctuation corrections. Deliver the revised passage with the unresolved date visibly retained or queried, plus the style entry for the refrain.

## Failure Case

Failure: replace every refrain with a synonym or silently choose the more plausible date. Correction: preserve rhetorical intent and distinguish factual uncertainty from copyediting authority.
