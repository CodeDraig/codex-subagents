## Proposal Context

- **P1 / O2 — painted metal object**
  - **Evidence:** R1 records green deposits from an image. R2 describes the surface as stable but gives no examination method.
  - **Significance:** The owner note states that earlier paint layers may be historically significant. Object use is not recorded.
  - **Unknowns:** Metal alloy, paint-layer composition and sequence, identity of the green material, prior treatments, and responsible conservator are unidentified.
  - **Desired outcome:** Reveal an asserted “original appearance.”
  - **Proposed intervention:** Remove the green layer using cleaning solution C.
  - **Expected change:** Loss of the green layer and greater visibility of an underlying surface; which layer would be revealed is unverified.

- **P2 / W3 — wall at a historic site**
  - **Evidence:** F1 describes a visible crack photographed with a scale. F2 says the crack appears unchanged in a more distant photograph without a scale.
  - **Significance:** W3 is identified as historic-site fabric. Its use, construction history, and significance of the joint material are not recorded.
  - **Unknowns:** Crack dimensions and behavior, mortar composition, loading, structural condition, prior repairs, and responsible conservation or structural professional.
  - **Desired outcome:** Address the crack and establish structural safety.
  - **Proposed intervention:** Remove and replace joint material, followed by structural-safety certification.

Both proposals request documentary review only. Neither contains intervention authorization from a qualified professional.

## Treatment Review

- **P1: Limited support.** R1 supports only that an image showed green deposits; it does not identify those deposits as corrosion, an applied layer, contamination, or material suitable for removal. R2’s “stable” description is not traceable to a stated examination method.
- **P1: Unsupported appearance claim.** No paint stratigraphy, historical reference, or material identification establishes which appearance is original or intended. Removing the green layer could expose an earlier or later paint layer; the owner note makes that distinction significant.
- **P1: Compatibility unresolved.** No test on O2 or a representative system addresses interactions among solution C, the unidentified alloy, paint layers, and green deposits. Evaporation from an empty glass dish does not demonstrate safe application, residue-free removal, or preservation of paint and metal.
- **P1: Reversibility claim unsupported.** The liquid’s evaporation from glass may be observable, but removal of the green layer would itself not be reversed by evaporation. Possible extraction, pigment change, altered gloss, residues, or redistribution on O2 have not been examined. What could later be removed is unknown; removed material and surface changes may remain changed. Future retreatment is not considered.

- **P2: Crack presence supported, stability unsupported.** F1 supports the presence of a crack. F2 is not a comparable follow-up because it was taken farther away and without a scale. The statement that the crack did not change is therefore an inference, not a measurement.
- **P2: Intervention rationale incomplete.** No evidence establishes that defective joint material caused the crack or that replacing it would address structural movement.
- **P2: Material compatibility unresolved.** No mortar analysis, masonry characterization, moisture assessment, or prior-repair record supports removal or replacement of the existing joint material. Potential loss of historic material and incompatibility with adjacent masonry remain unresolved risks.
- **P2: Safety claim unsupported.** Joint replacement cannot establish structural safety without a competent structural examination addressing loading, movement, wall configuration, and related fabric. The proposal does not support certification.

## Evidence Gaps

- **P1 / O2**
  - Direct examination documenting deposit distribution, adhesion, active change, and condition of exposed paint.
  - Identification or bounded characterization of the metal, paint layers, green material, and prior coatings or treatments.
  - Evidence distinguishing original, later, and historically significant appearances.
  - Object-relevant compatibility testing with documented methods, locations, observations, and acceptance criteria.
  - A treatment baseline with comparable photographs, scale, lighting, color reference, and mapped condition.
  - Evidence about residues, lasting surface change, and future retreatment.

- **P2 / W3**
  - Comparable crack measurements over time, using fixed locations, scale, orientation, and stated dates.
  - Structural examination and assessment of loads, movement, wall configuration, and associated defects.
  - Mortar and masonry characterization, including evidence of prior repointing or repair.
  - Inspection for moisture, drainage, foundation, vegetation, thermal, or other possible contributors.
  - Defined intervention extent and evidence that joint replacement addresses the diagnosed cause.
  - Baseline and follow-up criteria capable of distinguishing treatment effects from continuing movement.

## Alternatives And Tradeoffs

- **P1 deferral and monitoring:** Preserves current material while significance and compatibility remain unresolved. It delays the desired visual change and requires a repeatable condition baseline.
- **P1 further examination before deciding:** Could distinguish deposits and paint layers and clarify whether change is active. It requires qualified resources and does not guarantee that removal will become supportable.
- **P1 smaller intervention or authorized representative testing:** Could limit exposure while producing substrate-relevant evidence. Even a small test may permanently alter significant material and requires prior significance, location, and acceptance decisions.
- **P1 preventive measures:** Investigation of storage, display, handling, pollutants, and moisture could address continuing causes without removing material. Causes are currently unresolved, so measurements would need to precede material-sensitive changes.

- **P2 deferral with comparable monitoring:** Avoids immediate loss of historic joint material and can establish whether movement is occurring. It does not establish present structural safety; access or use controls may still require a competent risk decision.
- **P2 structural and fabric investigation:** Better addresses the safety objective and tests whether joint failure is causal. It requires appropriate qualified professionals and may identify a different intervention need.
- **P2 localized rather than general joint intervention:** Could reduce material loss if later supported by examination. Its appropriateness and extent cannot be determined from F1 and F2.
- **P2 site-level preventive measures:** Investigation of water management, vegetation, loading, and adjacent movement could address causes without assuming mortar replacement is corrective. Implementing changes remains contingent on evidence and custodian approval.

Across both proposals, alternatives should be compared using the same criteria: preservation of significant material, relevance to the diagnosed condition, compatibility, degree of permanent change, ability to monitor outcomes, and future retreatment.
