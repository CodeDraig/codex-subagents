## Treatment Review

**P1**

- **Supported:** R1 supports only that a green feature was identified from an image. It does not establish composition, extent, activity, cause, or whether the material is corrosion, a coating, pigment, deposit, or historically significant surface. R2’s stability statement has limited evidential weight because no examination method or coverage is recorded. [P1/R1/R2—packet.md:5](/tmp/heritage-fieldwork-20260906/live/conservation_treatment/attempt-2/project/inputs/packet.md:5)
- **Unsupported inference:** Removing the green layer would not necessarily reveal an “original” appearance. Paint stratigraphy and the historical status of each layer are unknown, while the owner note identifies a plausible significance constraint.
- **Compatibility:** No evidence addresses solution C’s interaction with the unidentified alloy, paint layers, interfaces, corrosion products, or previous materials. Evaporation from an empty glass dish is not a substrate-relevant compatibility, residue, clearance, or ageing test.
- **Change and retreatment:** The green material itself would be removed and could not be restored as the same material. Paint, metal, interfaces, gloss, color, texture, or corrosion behavior may remain changed even if the liquid carrier evaporates. No future retreatment implications are considered. “Fully reversible” is therefore unsupported.
- **Finding:** The desired appearance is stated, but the proposed intervention is not evidentially supported for O2’s unidentified material system and unresolved significance.

**P2**

- **Supported:** F1 documents a visible crack with a scale at one photographic moment. [P2/F1/F2—packet.md:7](/tmp/heritage-fieldwork-20260906/live/conservation_treatment/attempt-2/project/inputs/packet.md:7)
- **Unsupported inference:** F2 cannot demonstrate that the crack was unchanged because it was taken from farther away and lacks a scale. No measured widths or otherwise comparable observations are supplied.
- **Intervention basis:** Removing and replacing joint material is unsupported without identifying the existing and adjacent materials, understanding whether the joint is historic or previously replaced, and examining the crack’s movement and structural context.
- **Safety claim:** Repointing cannot itself establish structural safety. No load assessment or structural examination supports certification, and no qualified professional is identified to make that determination.
- **Change and retreatment:** Removed joint material cannot be reinstalled unchanged. Replacement may alter moisture movement, stress distribution, appearance, interfaces, and evidential fabric. Later removal would require another intervention and might leave interface loss or damage. Future retreatment is not addressed.
- **Finding:** The crack warrants assessment, but neither intervention nor structural certification follows from the supplied evidence.

## Checks And Limits

- Review coverage was limited to the synthetic packet, specifically P1/R1/R2 at line 5 and P2/F1/F2 at line 7. No underlying photographs, examination forms, specifications, test sheets, or earlier records were supplied.
- No physical examination, treatment, destructive testing, structural assessment, or material testing was performed in this review.
- P1’s empty-dish evaporation report was considered only as reported evidence of evaporation under unspecified conditions. It does not verify removability, absence of residue, compatibility, stability, or harmlessness on O2.
- R2’s stability claim cannot be independently evaluated because its method, date, examiner, conditions, and coverage are missing.
- F1 and F2 do not provide a comparable baseline and follow-up series. Their different distance and scale conditions prevent a supported no-change finding.
- Documentary completeness would not itself establish that either intervention is appropriate. On the supplied record, neither proposal is supported for intervention or safety certification.
