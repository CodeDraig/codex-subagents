## Concept Options

1. **Borrowed goodbye** — A grieving customer rents a memory advertised as their late mother’s final walk, then seeks a refund because it contains the customer’s own forgotten abandonment. Engine: grief becomes evidence.

2. **Contaminated inventory** — A customer says the rental implanted a stranger’s memories; increasingly specific annotations reveal the “stranger” is the shop’s missing former clerk. Engine: consumer complaint becomes identity mystery.

3. **The nonreturnable self** — A customer disputes a charge after returning a memory “unused,” while clerk notes establish that the memory was of the customer’s former self and has already changed them. Engine: refund policy collides with irreversible experience.

## Chosen Working Direction

Use **The nonreturnable self**. It fits four short receipts because each transaction can disclose one new fact: rental, attempted return, refund dispute, final refusal. The customer’s claim of nonuse remains plausible until annotations show they are reacting to a recovered memory rather than faulty merchandise.

Reversible assumption: the recovered memory concerns a person the customer left behind; the relationship and exact event remain undecided until drafting.
