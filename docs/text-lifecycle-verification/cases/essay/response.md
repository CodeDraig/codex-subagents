## Essay

At my kitchen table, I repaired an old radio alone. The tuning knob was cracked, a break across the part I needed to turn. I kept returning to it, not because it explained what was wrong, but because it made the work feel exposed. A radio can seem like a sealed object until it refuses to answer; then every piece asks to be noticed.

I found a loose wire and replaced it. After that, I turned the cracked knob again, slowly, and found a station. The change was almost too small to announce itself: an old object receiving something from beyond the table. I had been working toward a sound, but the sound did not feel like an ending. It felt like an invitation to attend.

Listening is often described as passive, as if it asks only that I keep still. The repair suggested another kind of listening: patient enough to notice a break, active enough to make room for what has not yet come through. The cracked knob remains part of that lesson. It does not make the radio more complete. It makes contact require care.

I keep the knob now. In my hand, the crack is a reminder that reception is not guaranteed, even when a station can be found. I can turn it and hear what arrives. I cannot say what, if anything, will remain once I stop turning.
