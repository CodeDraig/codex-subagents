Manuscript Draft

This exploratory observational study examined whether adult volunteers’ self-reported daily reading time changed over a two-week observation period. Twenty-four adult volunteers reported their reading time during the study period. Mean reported reading time increased by 3 minutes per day from the beginning to the end of the two weeks. This descriptive result indicates an observed upward change in participants’ reports over the observation window; it does not establish that reading behavior changed for reasons attributable to the study or to any specific exposure. Interpretation is limited by the small volunteer sample, reliance on self-reported reading time, short follow-up, and absence of a control group. The supplied results include neither uncertainty intervals nor significance tests, so the precision of the estimated change and its compatibility with chance variation cannot be assessed. Accordingly, the finding should be treated as exploratory and restricted to this sample and measurement context. Further research using a comparison group, prespecified measurement procedures, and statistical estimates of uncertainty would be needed to evaluate the robustness and generalizability of the observed increase.
